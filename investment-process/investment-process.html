01 · The pitch

Positioning

If the objective is to convince a client to hand over capital to manage in Bangladesh equities, the pitch doesn't start with individual stocks. It starts by building a professional investment-management proposition, in this order: credibility → investment philosophy → risk framework → track record → portfolio construction → reporting → fees → legal/compliance structure.

An important distinction: giving investment advice and actually managing another person's portfolio under discretionary authority can carry different regulatory implications in Bangladesh. Treat BSEC as the primary regulatory reference point before accepting client money or discretionary authority — BSEC itself provides investor education covering securities analysis and portfolio management. See Regulatory Note, and the Startup Plan for the licensing route in detail.

You need a simple answer to one question

"Why should I give you my money instead of investing myself?" Your answer should not be "I know which stocks will go up." It should read more like this:

"I run a disciplined, research-driven Bangladesh equity strategy focused on capital preservation, fundamental valuation and long-term compounding. I construct concentrated portfolios only when the expected return adequately compensates for the downside risk, and I provide transparent performance and risk reporting."

That's a far more credible proposition than a stock tip — and it's the one a client with real capital can actually evaluate.

One closing point about Bangladesh specifically: don't sell "Bangladesh stocks are going to rise" — markets can stay weak for long periods. Sell "regardless of what the market does, we have a disciplined framework for determining what to own, how much to own, when the thesis is wrong, and how much risk we're willing to take." That's the foundation of a credible portfolio-management business.

Kill criteria — decide these before you buy

Specify in advance: "I will exit if these fundamental assumptions are invalidated." For example:

  • EPS declines >30% without a temporary explanation
  • A major governance problem emerges
  • Debt increases beyond a defined threshold
  • ROE deteriorates
  • The investment thesis changes
  • Valuation becomes excessive

Kill criteria are extremely important: they demonstrate you're not emotionally attached to stocks — the exit conditions were set before the money was, not rationalized after a loss.

07 · Prove it, don't assert it

Build a Real Track Record

This is probably the single biggest thing needed before asking someone to entrust significant capital to you. If a verified professional track record doesn't exist yet, don't manufacture one — start a transparent model portfolio instead.

Model portfolio
"Bangladesh Alpha Portfolio" — starting capital Tk 10,000,000, benchmarked against the DSEX.
Record every
Buy, sell, dividend, transaction cost, cash position, and portfolio value — nothing summarized after the fact.

Then calculate, and publish, all of it

Absolute returnCAGRBenchmark returnAlpha VolatilitySharpe ratioSortino ratioMaximum drawdown Win rateAverage gainAverage lossTurnover

Most importantly: show drawdowns

A portfolio manager who only shows winning trades looks like a salesman. A portfolio manager who says:

"The portfolio returned 18.2% versus DSEX 9.7%, but experienced a maximum drawdown of 12.4%."

looks far more credible — the drawdown figure is what tells a client you're being honest about risk, not just performance.

Don't cherry-pick

If a portfolio holds 20 stocks and 8 were spectacular, don't show those eight — show the entire portfolio. A client should be able to trace the whole chain:

Starting capital → contributions/withdrawals → transactions → dividends → fees → ending value

Ideally, an independent broker/DP/CDBL record supports the holdings shown — see Custody & Trust for why that independent verification matters for building trust.

08 · The biggest trust signal

Custody & Trust

From a trust and operational-risk perspective, the structure should be designed so the client knows: "My shares are mine. My money is mine. The manager has defined authority to execute/manage according to the agreement."

CDBL provides BO (Beneficiary Owner) account services and portfolio valuation/balance information, and securities are normally held in the investor's own BO account rather than simply handed over to the portfolio manager. CDBL states that an investor normally has an account in their own name, with the rights and duties defined through the agreement with the CDBL participant — that distinction is central to building trust.

🚩 Avoid building your business around this
"Send me Tk 20 million into my personal bank account and I'll invest it."

That is an enormous red flag — to a sophisticated client and to a regulator. Instead, establish the appropriate regulated/legal structure and have the client's securities and cash handled through the proper intermediaries (their own BO account, a licensed broker/DP, and — for pooled capital — a licensed custodian/trustee; see the Startup Plan's Licensing section).

14 · Don't pressure them to invest

Your First Client Meeting

Structure a 45-minute meeting roughly like this — then stop. Don't pressure them to invest immediately.

0–5 min
Understand the client's objectives.
5–10 min
Ask about their existing experience.
10–15 min
Explain the Bangladesh market opportunity.
15–25 min
Explain your investment philosophy.
25–35 min
Show your research process and one complete stock analysis.
35–40 min
Show your historical/model portfolio results.
40–45 min
Explain risk, custody, reporting and fees.

Have precise answers ready — a sophisticated client will ask

  • "What happens if the market falls 30%?"
  • "What is your maximum position size?"
  • "What is your worst historical drawdown?"
  • "Why should I trust your valuation?"
  • "Who holds my shares?"
  • "What happens if you stop managing my portfolio?"
  • "What happens if I want my money back?"
15 · If you're starting with nothing

Roadmap From Zero

If a verifiable portfolio-management track record doesn't exist yet, the first objective isn't collecting Tk 100 million — it's earning the right to be trusted with any capital at all.

Stage 1 — Build CredibilityMonths 0–3

Research framework, DSE database, stock-ranking system, model portfolio, investment memos, monthly reports, website/dashboard.

Stage 2 — Establish Track RecordMonths 3–12

Run a transparent model portfolio. Publish monthly return, DSEX return, alpha, drawdown, holdings, and investment decisions — every month, without exception.

Stage 3 — First ClientsOnce credible

Start with people who understand your process and risk. Don't immediately target strangers with large amounts of capital.

Stage 4 — ScaleOnce ready

Once a track record, legal structure, compliance, reporting, operations, client agreement, and custody arrangements all exist — then approach larger investors.
