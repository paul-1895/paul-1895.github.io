What it is

Mark Minervini is a US equity trader known for a growth approach he calls SEPA — Specific Entry Point Analysis — and for the two-time US Investing Championship results that made it well known. The Trend Template is the first stage of that approach: a seven-condition filter that a stock must satisfy before it is even considered. Its job is to narrow thousands of stocks down to those already in a confirmed uptrend.

The Trend Template is a screen, not a strategy. It has no entry, no stop, no target and no position size. In Minervini's own method it is followed by fundamental screening, a specific entry pattern and strict risk management — none of which this app implements. That distinction is the single most important thing on this page, and a section below is devoted to it.

This app implements the template in routes/minervini-engine.js, evaluates it on every DSE stock on daily, weekly and monthly bars, and shows the results on the Minervini Model page. It has also been backtested properly, and that result is not flattering.

Every number on this page is served by the app's own engine and backtest file — none of it is written into this page.

The shape it describes

Strip the seven conditions down and they describe one picture: a stock that has finished basing, turned up, and now has price above a short average, above a medium average, above a long average, with the averages themselves stacked in that same order and the long one rising. The diagram below plots a price path with real moving averages computed from it — the stacking is a consequence of the prices, not three lines drawn in a flattering order.

What this picture cannot tell you is what happens next. Every chart that later doubled passed through this shape; so did every chart that topped out here and halved. The template identifies a state, and states are not forecasts — a point the backtest makes rather forcefully on this market.

Three timeframes

The template is defined in trading days: 50/150/200-day averages, about a month of slope, a 52-week range. Applying it to weekly and monthly bars means scaling those periods to their calendar equivalents, which this app does explicitly rather than reusing the daily numbers on different bars.

The monthly row is a compromise and the engine says so in its own source. A true 200-day average needs roughly ten months of monthly bars, and only a handful of stocks in this archive carry enough of them. Monthly results rest on about thirteen evaluable bars per stock and should be read as indicative — which matters, because monthly is the one timeframe whose backtest result comes out positive.

What the backtest found

For every bar where all seven criteria could be fully evaluated, the forward return over the following period was recorded. "Spread" is the mean forward return of passing bars minus that of non-passing bars, in percentage points. Positive means passing stocks went on to do better. This is the same engine the live page runs, with no lookahead.

The Super Model reached the same conclusion independently. When scripts/compare-candidates.js fitted signal weights across four separate time windows, a full Minervini pass came out with a weight of −20 — the largest single weight in the shipped model, and negative. It was the only finding that held at every split. On this market and this sample, extended uptrends mean-reverted.

What isn't implemented — and why that limits the finding

The backtest tests the Trend Template. Minervini's actual method is considerably more than the Trend Template, and none of the rest of it exists in this codebase. Anyone reading the negative result above as "Minervini's approach doesn't work on the DSE" is claiming more than the evidence supports.

Part of the methodWhat it addsIn this app?
Relative strength ranking Minervini requires a stock to be in the top tier of performance versus other stocks, not merely rising on its own. No. The template as coded here compares each stock only to its own history.
Fundamental screening Accelerating earnings and sales growth, margin expansion — the "specific" part of Specific Entry Point Analysis. No. The engine reads price bars only.
Volatility Contraction Pattern The entry setup: successive tighter pullbacks on falling volume, bought on the breakout from the final contraction. No. Nothing here detects contractions or volume dry-up.
Hard stop-loss discipline Minervini's published approach cuts losses at a small fixed percentage, so the average loss stays far below the average gain. No — and this is the big one. The backtest measures forward returns of a state. With no stop, the losing tail is carried in full, which is precisely what the risk rules exist to remove.
Position sizing and exits How much to buy, when to add, when to take profit. No. There is no portfolio being simulated anywhere in this backtest.
So what has been established? That on this archive, being in the state the Trend Template describes was not followed by better-than-average returns — and was more often followed by worse. That is a real result about the screen. It is not a test of SEPA, and this page should not be read as one.

Reading it honestly

A screen designed for one market was tested on another

The template was developed on US equities — thousands of stocks, deep liquidity, decades of history, and a market where sustained multi-year growth leaders are common. The DSE has around 400 listings, many of them thinly traded, and this archive covers a period dominated by a floor-price regime and its unwinding. Both the rule and the market matter to the result, and only one of them was Minervini's.

The sample is much shorter than the observation count suggests

Tens of thousands of observations sound decisive, but consecutive daily bars share nearly all of their forward window, so they are not independent. The usable universe only has broad price history from around May 2025. Read it as roughly a fourteen-month test with heavy overlap, not as a decade of evidence.

One two-month window carries most of the result

The period breakdown is on the page above rather than buried, because it changes the reading materially: exclude that single window and every timeframe sits within about half a point of zero. The honest summary is "no demonstrated edge", which is weaker and more accurate than "it works in reverse".

Two negative results here are not two pieces of evidence

The Super Model's −20 weight and this backtest lean on overlapping data and the same episode. They agree because they are looking at much the same thing, which is a weaker form of confirmation than two independent studies agreeing.

Do not invert it either

A negative spread is not a shorting signal. The direction fitted on a training period reversed depending on where the split fell, which is the signature of noise rather than of an inverted edge — and short selling is not generally available on the DSE anyway. "No demonstrated edge in either direction" is where this ends.

The template still describes something real

A passing stock genuinely is above its averages, near its high and well off its low. That is a useful factual description of where a stock sits, and it is a reasonable way to sort a watchlist. What the evidence does not support is treating that description as a prediction.
