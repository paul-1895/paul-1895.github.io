What it is

Hilega-Milega is not a standard indicator you will find in a textbook — it is a custom pane this app draws, built entirely out of one input: a 9-period RSI. Everything else on the pane is a smoothing of that same RSI line. Understanding that one fact explains most of how it behaves.

RSI(9) The fast line. A 9-period RSI of closing price — quicker and noisier than the standard RSI(14) drawn in its own pane above.
WMA(21) The dotted "strength" line: a 21-period weighted moving average of the RSI, not of price. This is the slow reference the fast line is measured against.
EMA(3) The "price" line: a 3-period EMA of the RSI. Barely a smoothing at all — it tracks the RSI closely and mostly serves to make the turn direction easier to see.
Line-50 The RSI midpoint. The shaded fill flips colour across it, which is what gives the pane its at-a-glance bullish/bearish read.

How the pane is built

Three lines, computed in order. Each step takes the output of the one above it — which is why the whole pane moves together and why none of the lines can disagree with each other for long.

StepComputed fromConsequence
RSI(9) Closing prices, 9 bars. Bounded 0–100, so it never trends off the top of the pane the way price does.
WMA(21) The RSI series, 21 bars, linearly weighted toward the newest value. A smoothing of a smoothing. It lags the RSI by construction, which is precisely what makes a crossover possible.
EMA(3) The RSI series, 3 bars. So short it is nearly the RSI itself; it is a readability aid, not an independent signal.
Why the warm-up is longer than it looks. The RSI needs 9 bars before it produces anything, and the WMA then needs 21 more RSI values on top of that. During the gap, attachHilegaMilega() substitutes 50 for the missing RSI values so the average has something to chew on — so the first stretch of the dotted line is anchored to a placeholder, not to real data. Do not read crossovers from the left edge of a freshly loaded chart.

Zones versus crossovers

The pane tells you two different things, and they are easy to confuse. The shading is a zone read — which side of 50 the RSI sits on. The crossover is an event — the fast line passing its own average. They often disagree, and only one of them is what the Screener scans for.

Same pane, two readings. Orange fill means RSI is above 50 — momentum leaning bullish. But the RSI can be well above 50 and still cross below its WMA(21), which the Screener reports as a bearish signal. A stock can therefore be "in the orange" and appear in the sell list on the same day. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions.

What this app actually detects

The Screener's Hilega-Milega Buy / Sell lists come from detectHmCrossover() in routes/screener-technicals.js. It is a strict two-bar test on the most recent completed daily bar only:

SignalConditionNote
Buy prev: RSI − WMA ≤ 0 and now: RSI − WMA > 0 Crossed up on this bar. A stock already above its WMA does not qualify — the list is fresh crossings only.
Sell prev: RSI − WMA ≥ 0 and now: RSI − WMA < 0 Crossed down on this bar.

Note the ≤ and ≥ on the previous bar: a bar where the two lines are exactly equal counts as "not yet above", so a touch-and-go still registers as a cross the next bar. Only the daily timeframe is scanned — unlike the MACD scan, there is no weekly or monthly option.

Expect a lot of them. Because it fires on any crossing of a fast line and its own short average, this is one of the busiest scans in the app — on a typical day it returns dozens of names across the ~396 listed stocks. A signal that common cannot also be rare and precious; treat it as a shortlist to look at, not a list to act on.

What the backtest found

This is the part worth reading twice. Hilega-Milega was not taken on trust — the Super Model tested it as a candidate signal against forward returns, and it did not survive.

MeasureResultMeaning
Edge, earlier half+0.75 Excess forward return of bullish over bearish crossings.
Edge, later half−0.15 The sign flipped when the history was cut at a different date.
Consistent?No An effect that changes direction with the split is the signature of noise, not edge.
Weight in the shipped model0 Detected and displayed, but contributes nothing to any score.
What that does and does not mean. It does not prove Hilega-Milega is worthless — the sample is roughly 13 months of broad-market DSE history, which is thin, and a signal can be useful inside a strategy while showing no edge in isolation. What it does mean is that on this data, nobody has demonstrated it works, and the model that could have used it chose not to. That is the honest status: unproven here, not disproven, and certainly not confirmed.

For contrast, only four signals earned a non-zero weight in that model: the 50-day SMA trend (+12), Supertrend (+12), monthly seasonality (+8), and a full Minervini pass at −20 — scored negatively. MACD, liquidity sweeps, support/resistance proximity and Hilega-Milega were all measured and all zeroed.

Reading it honestly

Before the general points, one specific thing you can see. Every ring below is a bar where the crossover test fires. This stretch is the same series from the top of the page — – signals in – bars, alternating buy and sell almost every other session while price went essentially nowhere.

This is the failure mode, not an edge case. When the RSI hovers near its own average, the sign of a tiny difference flips repeatedly — and each flip is a fresh entry in the Screener's list. Acting on every one of these would have meant a round-trip in commission every second day for no directional move at all. It is the clearest reason the crossover cannot be used on its own.

It is an oscillator read, not a price read

Every line on this pane derives from RSI, and RSI derives from the ratio of average gains to average losses. A bullish crossover says momentum improved relative to its own recent average — price can still be falling while that happens.

Smoothing a smoothing adds lag, not information

WMA(21) of RSI(9) contains nothing RSI(9) did not already contain; it is the same series, delayed. The crossover is therefore an artefact of that delay. That is not a criticism — it is what makes the event well-defined — but it is worth knowing no new data enters the pane after the first line.

The diagrams here are synthetic — deliberately

Every plot on this page runs the indicator's real maths over a generated price series, not a real stock, so the shapes stay stable as the archive grows. The generator is calibrated against the archive: across 57 DSE stocks over 130 bars, RSI(9) had a median range of 10–97 and a median 11 bullish / 10 bearish crossings — this series produces 12–100 and 11 / 11. It behaves like your charts, but it is not one of them. For a real stock, open the chart page.

The app itself declines to rely on it

The Super Model detects this signal, shows it in its reasoning, and gives it a weight of zero. When a tool built on the same codebase measures a signal and then refuses to score it, that is the most direct evidence available to you — and it is worth more than any pattern's reputation.
