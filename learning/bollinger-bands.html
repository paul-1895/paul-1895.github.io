What it is

Bollinger Bands are a volatility envelope: a moving average in the middle, and an upper and lower band a fixed number of standard deviations away from it. Unlike MACD or RSI, the bands don't produce a single "line crossed line" event — they describe a range price has recently tended to stay inside, and how wide or narrow that range currently is.

Middle band A 20-period SMA of closing price. The basis every other line is measured against.
Upper / lower bands The middle band ± 2 standard deviations of price over the same 20 bars. The shaded region between them is the "normal" range for that stock's recent volatility — it is not a fixed width, it moves with the stock.

How it's computed

Ported directly from this app's own indicators/bollinger-bands.js and indicators/ma.js.

StepComputed fromConsequence
Middle band SMA(20) of closing price. A simple, equally-weighted average — no null-padding tricks, just no value for the first 19 bars.
Standard deviation The same 20-bar window's population variance — divided by 20, not 19. A common point of divergence between Bollinger implementations is population vs. sample variance; this app uses population (÷N). If you compare band width against another platform's default and it looks slightly different, this is a likely reason why.
Upper / lower band Middle band ± (2 × that standard deviation). The multiplier (2) and period (20) are both this app's fixed defaults — there is no settings panel to change them, unlike MACD's configurable fast/slow/signal lengths.

Squeeze & expansion

Because the bands are built from a standard deviation, their width is a direct reading of recent volatility. A "squeeze" — the bands pulling tight — means the stock has been unusually quiet; an "expansion" right after means that quiet broke. The marked point below is the single narrowest moment in this page's own generated series, found by measuring every bar rather than picked by eye.

A squeeze says "something's coming," not which way. The bands narrowing tells you volatility has compressed and statistically tends to expand again — it says nothing about direction. Traders who act on a squeeze alone are betting on a breakout, not picking a side, and need a separate reason to guess which way it breaks.

How it's commonly read

These are general, widely taught readings of Bollinger Bands — not claims about what this app validates. See what this app actually does with it below for that distinction.

ReadingIdeaCommon caveat
Mean reversion Price touching the upper/lower band is "stretched" and likely to pull back toward the middle band. Works in range-bound conditions; fails outright in a strong trend (see "band walking" below).
Band walking In a strong trend, price can ride along the upper (or lower) band for many bars in a row rather than reverting. This is the direct counter-case to mean reversion — the same touch means opposite things depending on the regime, and the bands alone don't tell you which regime you're in.
Squeeze breakout A narrow-band period often precedes a bigger move; some traders position ahead of the expected expansion. Timing which bar the breakout actually starts on, and which direction, is the hard part this reading doesn't solve.

What this app actually detects

Nothing, as a scannable signal — and this section says so plainly rather than implying otherwise. Confirmed by grepping screener/screener.js, routes/screener-technicals.js, and both Super Model source files: zero matches for Bollinger Bands in any of them.

Reading it honestly

There is nothing to report here — and that itself is worth saying

Hilega-Milega and MACD + EMA both have a section on this Hub showing a real, measured result — in both cases, an unflattering one. This guide can't do that for Bollinger Bands, because nothing has ever been run. "Untested" and "tested and found wanting" are different claims; don't read this page as evidence either way.

A band touch is a description, not an event

MACD crossing its signal line is a single, well-defined bar you can point to. "Price touched the upper band" is true on every bar it stays true for — there's no natural single moment to test the way a crossover has one. That ambiguity is part of why no screener list exists for it here.

Mean reversion and band-walking directly contradict each other

Both are standard, widely taught readings of the same event (price at a band), and they predict opposite outcomes. Which one applies depends on a trend judgment the bands themselves don't make — so acting on a touch alone means silently picking one of two contradictory rules and hoping the regime matches.
