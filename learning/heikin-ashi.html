What it is

Heikin-Ashi ("average bar" in Japanese) is not a new price series — it's a smoothing transform applied on top of ordinary OHLC candles. Each Heikin-Ashi (HA) candle's open, high, low and close are all built from averages of the real candle(s) around it, which trades a bit of price precision for a much cleaner read on trend direction.

The core trade-off in one line: HA candles make trends easier to see and choppy periods easier to spot — at the cost of the candle no longer showing you a real price anyone actually traded at. See Reading it honestly for why that matters.

How it's computed

Ported directly from this app's own candlestick_chart/candlestick-data.js, function calculateHeikinAshi().

FieldFormulaConsequence
HA Close (Open + High + Low + Close) ÷ 4 — the average of all four real prices on that bar. Always sits near the middle of the real bar's range, which is part of why HA closes lag real closes at sharp turns.
HA Open (previous HA Open + previous HA Close) ÷ 2. The very first bar falls back to (real Open + real Close) ÷ 2, since there's no prior HA bar to average. This is the recursive part — every HA open carries a memory of every bar before it, which is what makes a run of same-color HA candles look so clean.
HA High max(real High, HA Open, HA Close) Guarantees the HA body and its wick stay consistent with each other, even though neither endpoint is a real traded price.
HA Low min(real Low, HA Open, HA Close) Same guarantee on the downside.

Reading the candles

These three cases are hand-built OHLC runs, not sampled from a random series — chosen so each one unambiguously shows the regime it's labelled as. Every HA candle drawn is the real formula above run on the raw candles beside it.

The pattern to look for is the wick, not just the color. Long same-color bodies with little or no wick on the "against trend" side signal conviction; small bodies with wicks on both sides signal indecision — that second case is the closest thing HA has to its own "reversal candle."

Strategies with different indicators

Heikin-Ashi answers "what's the trend doing right now" — it doesn't by itself answer "should I enter," "where's my stop," or "is this overextended." That's normally filled in with a second indicator. These are widely used combinations, not claims about what this app has tested (see what this app actually does for that distinction).

HA + Moving Average

Trend filter

The idea Only act on an HA color flip when it agrees with the slope of a longer moving average (e.g. 50 or 200 EMA) — the MA answers "which direction should I even be looking," HA answers "is that direction still intact right now."

Why pair it HA alone can stay green through a shallow pullback in a strong downtrend; a trend filter stops that from reading as a fresh buy signal.

In this app Chart Type → Heikin-Ashi and the MA overlay are both available on the same chart and drawn independently — enabling one doesn't disable the other.

HA + MACD

Momentum confirmation

The idea Wait for an HA color flip and a MACD signal-line crossover in the same direction close together, rather than acting on either alone.

Why pair it Both are lagging by construction — HA from its recursive averaging, MACD from its own moving averages — so this combination is deliberately built for confirmation, not for early entries.

In this app MACD is one of this chart's default sub-panes (see the MACD + EMA guide for how it's actually computed and tested here).

HA + RSI

Overextension check

The idea Use RSI to avoid taking a fresh HA trend signal when momentum is already extreme — e.g. skip a new HA-uptrend entry if RSI is already deep into overbought territory.

Why pair it HA's smoothing can make a trend look calm right up until it's exhausted; RSI is one of the few tools in this pairing that's actually measuring how stretched the move already is.

In this app RSI is the other default sub-pane alongside MACD, drawn from real closes regardless of which chart type is selected — see what this app actually does below.

HA + Supertrend

Stop placement

The idea Let HA's color decide direction and use an ATR-based Supertrend line as the actual trailing stop — HA doesn't produce a tradeable stop level on its own (see Reading it honestly).

Why pair it Supertrend gives a concrete, volatility-scaled number to act on, which is exactly what HA is missing by design.

In this app Supertrend is listed in this chart's own indicator catalog (AVAILABLE_INDICATORS in candlestick-data.js) as an "ATR-based trend indicator."

HA + Hilega-Milega

This app's own oscillator

The idea Use this app's RSI(9)/WMA(21) oscillator pane the same way as RSI above — a momentum cross-check underneath an HA trend read.

Why mention it It's not a textbook pairing anywhere else — it exists because this app built it. See its own guide for what a real backtest of it found.

In this app Read the Hilega-Milega guide for the exact formula and the honest backtest result.

Reading it honestly

An HA candle's price is not a price anyone traded at

The open, high, low and close on an HA candle are all averages. You cannot place a stop or a limit order "at" an HA level, because that number was never quoted by the exchange — this is the single most common mistake newcomers to HA make. Any stop or target still has to reference the real candle underneath.

Gaps disappear

Because HA Open is a running average of prior bars, a real overnight gap up or down gets smoothed away rather than shown. If a strategy depends on spotting gaps, it has to be run on the raw candles, not the HA ones.

It's a lagging transform, not a leading one

Both HA Open and HA Close are averages, and averages lag by construction. HA candles tend to confirm a reversal a bar or two after the raw candles already show one — the same trade-off every smoothing tool makes for a cleaner read.

The regime diagrams above are constructed, not sampled

Same reasoning as every other guide on this Hub: the three OHLC runs under "Reading the candles" were hand-built to each unambiguously show one regime, not picked at random from a generated series. The real-chart card further up is the only genuinely real-stock diagram on this page.
