What it is

Fibonacci retracement is not a computed indicator — it's a ruler. A trader picks two points on a chart (usually a swing high and a swing low), and the tool projects a fixed set of ratios between them as horizontal price levels. The idea is that pullbacks and extensions often pause near these particular ratios, borrowed from the Fibonacci sequence in mathematics. This app's chart page offers four such drawing tools — covered below — but draws nothing automatically. Every level on every chart starts from two or three clicks a person made.

This is the one guide on this Hub with no anatomy diagram of "the indicator computing itself." There's no ongoing calculation to show — only what happens once you've already chosen two points, which the worked example below covers instead.

The ratio sets

Ported directly from this app's own candlestick_chart/tv-drawing-tools.js — the exact fibLevels() arrays and price formulas the drawing tools use.

Tool familyRatios usedPrice formula
Retracement 0%, 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%, 100% p1 + (p2 − p1) × ratio, where p1/p2 are the two clicked prices, in click order.
Extension / Channel / Trend-Based Extension 0%, 38.2%, 61.8%, 100%, 127.2%, 161.8%, 200% p3 + (p2 − p1) × ratio — the size of the first leg (p2 − p1), projected forward from a third clicked point.
50% isn't actually a Fibonacci ratio. It's included because it's the single most widely watched retracement level in practice, not because it comes from the Fibonacci sequence the way 23.6%/38.2%/61.8%/78.6% do (each derived from ratios between consecutive or skip-one terms of the sequence). This app includes it because every major charting platform does, not because there's a mathematical reason for it here.

The four tools

All four live in the chart page's drawing toolbar, all four are entirely mouse-driven, and all four save to the same per-symbol drawing storage as trendlines and rectangles.

ToolClicksDraws
Fib Retracement 2 The 7 retracement levels between the two points, each labeled with its ratio and price.
Fib Extension 3 The 7 extension levels projected from the third point, sized by the distance between the first two.
Fib Channel 3 A parallel channel (like the plain Parallel Channel tool) with the retracement ratios drawn as dashed lines between the two channel boundaries, rather than as standalone horizontals.
Trend-Based Fib Extension 3 Same extension math as Fib Extension, plus the first leg (point 1 to point 2) is drawn faintly on the chart as a visual reference trendline.

How it's commonly read

General, widely taught readings — not claims this app has validated. There is no "what this app detects" section on this page, because nothing is detected; see Reading it honestly for why.

LevelCommon reading
38.2% – 61.8% Often called the "golden pocket" — the zone many traders watch for a pullback to find support (in an uptrend) or resistance (in a downtrend) before the prior trend resumes.
78.6% A "deep" retracement — some traders treat a pullback this far as a warning the original move may be failing rather than pausing.
127.2% / 161.8% Common extension targets for where a move might reach after a retracement completes — frequently used to set take-profit levels.

Reading it honestly

There is nothing here for this app to have tested

Hilega-Milega and MACD + EMA both report a real, measured backtest result — in both cases, an unflattering one. That is only possible because both have a mechanical, repeatable rule for what counts as a signal. Fibonacci levels start from a human choosing two points; there is no consistent rule to replay across history, so there is nothing to score. "Untested" is the honest and complete description here — not "unproven," not "disproven."

Swing-point selection is where all the real judgment is

The worked example above used the highest and lowest closes in a fixed 60-session window — one reasonable, disclosed choice. A different window length, using intraday highs/lows instead of closes, or picking a different swing entirely, all produce different levels from the same chart. The ratios are exact; the two points feeding them are not.

Widely watched levels can matter for a reason unrelated to the ratios themselves

If enough market participants place orders near the same well-known levels, those levels can influence price simply through crowding — a self-fulfilling mechanism, not evidence that 61.8% is mathematically special to markets. This app has no way to measure how much of that crowding effect, if any, applies to DSE-listed stocks specifically.

After-the-fact swing selection is close to unfalsifiable

Because any two points can be chosen once a move is already known, it's easy to find some swing whose retracement "explains" a reversal after it happened. That is a different and much weaker claim than predicting the swing and the reaction to it in advance — the distinction this whole page tries to keep clear.

See it live: open the drawing toolbar on any stock's chart page and try any of the four tools above. Related reading: Hilega-Milega, MACD + EMA, Bollinger Bands — for contrast, two of those three guides have real backtest evidence to show; this one, honestly, cannot.
