Anatomy of a candle

Every candle on every chart in this app is four numbers — open, high, low, close — drawn as a shape. Learning candles is really learning to read those four numbers quickly. Nothing else is encoded in the shape, and treating it as though more is there is the single most common mistake.

Body The distance between open and close. Filled green here when the close is higher, red when lower. Its size is the session's conviction.
Upper wick From the top of the body up to the high. This is territory price reached and could not hold — a record of supply.
Lower wick From the bottom of the body down to the low. Territory price reached and was bought back from — a record of demand.
Range High minus low: the whole bar. Almost every rule below is a ratio against this, which is what lets one rule work on a ৳20 stock and a ৳400 one alike.
The one idea worth keeping. A long body says one side won and held. A long wick says one side tried and failed. Body = conviction, wick = rejection. Nearly every named pattern below is a rearrangement of those two statements.

Percentages under each diagram are measured from the very OHLC numbers the shape is drawn from, so the picture and the figures cannot disagree.

Multi-candle patterns

These need two or three bars in sequence. None of them are detected anywhere in this app today — they are here to read by eye on the chart page.

What this app actually detects

The Screener scans every listed stock for exactly four candle shapes. The thresholds are constants at the top of screener/screener.js — reproduced here so the guide and the code cannot drift apart.

ConstantValueWhat it gates
CANDLE_MIN_RANGE_PCT0.3% Minimum high-to-low range. Below this the bar is noise and every ratio becomes unstable, so no pattern is reported at all.
HAMMER_MIN_WICK_RATIO2.0× The dominant wick must be at least twice the body.
HAMMER_MAX_OPP_RATIO10% The opposite wick must be near-absent, or the bar is a spinning top rather than a hammer.
HAMMER_MAX_BODY_RATIO35% Body must stay small relative to the range.
MARUBOZU_MIN_BODY_RATIO85% Body must dominate the range.
MARUBOZU_MAX_WICK_RATIO5% Each wick must be almost nothing.
An important quirk of the daily data. The Screener builds these candles with open = YCP (yesterday's closing price), not the session's real opening trade — the Screener's own footer says "open ≈ YCP". On a stock that gaps at the open, the body it measures is the gap plus the day's move, so a marubozu there may be describing the overnight jump rather than a session of one-way trade. The chart page uses true OHLC and can therefore disagree with the Screener about the same stock on the same day.

Chart types in this app

The chart page can redraw the same four numbers several different ways. Each transformation trades some information away for some clarity — knowing which is which keeps you from reading a level off a chart that never had one.

Reading them honestly

A pattern is a description, not a prediction

Every shape above describes what already happened in a session. None of them carry a probability of what happens next. The names — "reversal", "continuation" — are conventions from a different market and century, not measured outcomes on the DSE.

Context outranks shape, every time

The identical shape is a Hammer after a decline and a Hanging Man after a rally. The candle cannot tell you which; only the fifty bars to its left can. Any rule that fires on one bar in isolation is throwing that away.

This app has evidence that textbook structure can mislead

The Super Model scores a full 7/7 Minervini uptrend negatively — on this market and sample, that textbook-bullish structure was followed by underperformance in every period tested. That finding is about trend templates rather than candles, but the lesson transfers: a shape being canonical in the literature is not evidence it works here.

Higher timeframes carry more signal — for a concrete reason

A weekly bar aggregates five sessions of trading, so one unusual print moves its shape far less than it moves a daily bar's. That matters more on the DSE than on a deep market: on a thinly traded stock a single daily candle can represent a handful of trades, and its "pattern" is then an artefact of low volume rather than a balance of buyers and sellers. Aggregating up is a real defence — but it is a mechanism, not a guarantee, and it costs you timeliness.

Small ranges manufacture patterns

On an illiquid stock a ৳0.10 range can technically satisfy a hammer's ratios. That is why CANDLE_MIN_RANGE_PCT exists. When reading by eye, apply the same discipline: check the range is real before naming the shape.

Want to test any of this? The Screener lists today's hammers and marubozus across all ~396 listed stocks, and the chart page will show you the bars around them.
